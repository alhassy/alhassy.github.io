* Typical workflow: How do I publish an article?
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: typical-workflow-how-do-i-publish-an-article
:END:
 
1. Open an Org-mode buffer ---or call doc:blog-new-article (or doc:blog-new-post).

2. Invoke doc:my/blogging-mode to get live WYSIWYG in an adjacent buffer after every
   save 〔C-x C-s〕.
   - The mode is auto-enabled in =~/blog/= via =.dir-locals.el=.
     
3. Until content:
   1. ✏️ Write, write, and write!
      - E.g., insert an image with  〔C-c i i〕 (from file) or 〔C-c i s〕 (screenshot) 
   2. 👀 〔C-x C-s〕 to save & preview

   🤔 Consider using 〔C-x n s〕, or 〔C-x n n〕, to focus your attention on a
   particular section, /thereby/ dramatically increasing the speed at which the
   preview renders. 💥

4. 🤖 ~git push~ when you're done ---CI runs =blog-publish-all= and deploys
   =public/= to =gh-pages=, index, RSS, and tag pages included.
   - Changes go live about 2 minutes after the push; see §Deployment via CI.

** The Two Article Styles: =standalone= vs =multiple=
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-two-article-styles-standalone-vs-multiple
:END:

We support two ways to author blog posts, selectable per-file via =#+article_style:=.

*** =standalone= (the default)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: standalone-the-default
:END:

One =.org= file = one article; its file-level keywords (=#+title:=, =#+filetags:=, …)
supply the metadata.

*** =multiple= (container files)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: multiple-container-files
:END:

One =.org= file = N articles, one per /top-level heading/.  Starting a post is then
just =C-RET=, a few prompts, and writing ---no new file, slug, or keywords.

A container file looks like this:

#+begin_example org
,#+title: My Life Posts
,#+article_style: multiple

,* A Walk in the Park                       :life:
:PROPERTIES:
:DATE:        <2026-05-03>
:DESCRIPTION: An evening walk with the kids.
:IMAGE:       park.png 350 350
:END:

,** Abstract :ignore:
An evening walk with the kids ---the light was perfect.

,** The Walk
...

,* Quick Emacs Tip: visual-line-mode        :emacs:draft:
:PROPERTIES:
:DATE:        <2026-05-04>
:DESCRIPTION: One line, no scroll bar.
:CUSTOM_ID:   quick-emacs-tip-visual-line-mode
:END:
...

,* Private scratch                          :noexport:
...
#+end_example

Key rules:
- *Heading tags* → article tags, sans structural ones (=noexport=, =draft=, =ignore=, =details=, …).
- *=:draft:=* → a draft, unlisted; =blog-new-post= adds it, and we axe it when ready.  See §drafts.
- *=:noexport:= or =COMMENT=* → skipped entirely; never exported or indexed.
- *=:TITLE:=* → the display title and slug source, overriding a decorated heading.
- *=:CUSTOM_ID:=* → the URL slug; see §slugs.
- *Abstract* → =:ABSTRACT:=, else the =** Abstract :ignore:= child, else the first paragraph.

*** Slug derivation, write-back, and global uniqueness
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: slug-derivation-write-back-and-global-uniqueness
:END:

By way of example, the heading =* Quick Emacs Tip: visual-line-mode= is published at
=alhassy.com/quick-emacs-tip-visual-line-mode=.

- A container heading's slug is its =:CUSTOM_ID:=; 〔C-x C-s〕 stamps a missing one
  with =blog--make-slug= of the title, so later title edits never shift the URL.
- =blog--validate-slugs= refuses a slug that isn't lower-case kebab-case, or that another
  article ---container or standalone--- already owns, naming both.

Each heading is then just another post-alist, so =blog-make-index-page= needs no special case.

** Drafts: published, yet unlisted
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: drafts-published-yet-unlisted
:END:

A draft is an article we're still mulling over: it is published at its URL, so we can read it on
the go, yet it's unlisted ---absent from the index, the tag pages, and the RSS feeds.  An article is
a draft when any of these hold:

- its container heading is tagged =:draft:= ---as =blog-new-post= does--- or carries =:DRAFT: t=;
- its standalone file says =#+draft: t=; or
- its file lives under =~/blog/draft/=, by way of example =draft/half-baked-idea.org=; a container
  there makes every one of its headings a draft.

=@draft?= is the one predicate, and =blog--listed-posts= the one filter, behind the index, tag pages,
and RSS; =blog-posts= keeps drafts, so =blog--validate-slugs= still catches a draft whose slug clashes
with a published article.  A draft's page also opens with a banner saying so, and carries
=<meta name="robots" content="noindex">= lest a stray link let search engines list it.

To see every draft at once, visit =alhassy.com/drafts=: =blog-make-index-page= builds it from =@draft?=
posts, as cards like the index's.  It is itself unlisted ---no nav link, no feed, and =noindex=.

Publishing is then just axing the marker ---or moving the file out of =draft/=--- and saving; the
bumped =:MODIFIED:= makes it new on the index and in RSS that day.  Mind that unlisted isn't secret:
the =.org= source is on GitHub, and its colourised copy is deployed beside the article.

** REDIRECT: zero-duplication bridges to external Org files        :details:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: redirect-zero-duplication-bridges-to-external-org-files
:END:

Often a project's lovingly maintained =README.org=, or =~/.emacs.d/init.org=, /is/ the
article; copying it into a container heading is pointless.  Instead, give the heading a
=:REDIRECT:= of a local path or an =https://= URL: its body becomes a single =#+include:=
of that file, and Org does the rest.

#+begin_src org :tangle no
,* My Literate Emacs Config :emacs:lisp:
:PROPERTIES:
:DATE:        <2024-01-15>
:DESCRIPTION: A literate Emacs configuration, tangled from Org and readable as prose.
:REDIRECT:    ~/.emacs.d/init.org
:END:

:REDIRECT:    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alhassy/holy-books/main/holy-books.org
:END:

*** The repo owns the =.org=; the blog points at it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-repo-owns-the-org-the-blog-points-at-it
:END:

By way of example, holy-books is a literate Emacs package: =holy-books.org= tangles to
=holy-books.el=, which MELPA ships.  Its write-up belongs on the blog, where friends who'd
never browse GitHub find it via the index, tag pages, and RSS; its code belongs in its own
repo, where people star it, fork it, and file issues.

#+begin_example
github.com/alhassy/holy-books          blog (alhassy.github.io)
├── holy-books.org   ← single source   ├── projects.org
├── holy-books.el    ← tangled, committed     * Holy Books  :emacs:
├── tests.el                                   :REDIRECT: https://raw…/holy-books.org
├── README.md        ← tiny, hand-written      * Repl-Driven Dev  :emacs:
└── images/                                    :REDIRECT: …
#+end_example

Why not the other way round, with the blog owning the =.org= and tangling into
=~/holy-books/=?  Then the =:tangle= paths are absolute, the repo's CI can't re-tangle, and
every pull request against the =.el= is silently clobbered by our next tangle.  Contributors
fork one repo, and should find the literate source beside the code.

- *=projects.org=* is a container with one heading per repo, each a =:REDIRECT:=.  Its
  first heading carries =:SITE_NAV: Projects=, so one URL ---linked from every page--- lists
  everything; it's the link we send friends.  Adding a repo is a five-line heading.
- *=README.md=* in each repo is hand-written and tiny: a badge row, one GIF, a
  =use-package= snippet, a short table of the main commands, and "Full write-up →".  It
  changes rarely, so generating it from the =.org= (=ox-md= on a =:readme:= subtree) is a
  pipeline we don't need; in due time, if the command table starts drifting, we revisit.
- *=;; URL:=* in the package header points at the GitHub repo, not the blog, so bug reports
  land where the code lives.
- *Per-repo GitHub Pages*, like =alhassy.com/holy-books/=, are forthcoming retirees: each
  write-up should live at one canonical URL.  Until then, a redirected article's slug must
  differ from its repo's name, else the project site shadows it ---hence
  =:CUSTOM_ID: holy-books-in-org-mode=, not =holy-books=.

*Migrating a repo* is then a checklist:
1. Un-=COMMENT= its heading in =projects.org=, with a =:CUSTOM_ID:= unlike the repo's name.
2. Tag repo-only plumbing ---the package preamble, the test suite, the CI YAML---
   =:noexport:=: it still tangles, but never reaches readers.
3. Make the tangle a CI-checked fixpoint, lest someone patch the =.el= directly: CI
   re-tangles the =.org= and fails if =git diff --exit-code= finds the committed
   =.el= differs; see =holy-books.org='s "Testing" heading.
4. 〔C-x C-s〕 in =projects.org= and eyeball the preview.

*** A REDIRECT tracks =master=: vendoring, freshness, and staleness
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: vendoring-redirects-so-ci-can-resolve-them
:END:

A =:REDIRECT:= of =~/.emacs.d/init.org= works on our laptop and fails on CI, which has no
=~/.emacs.d/=; a URL fails whenever the network does.  So every publish /vendors/ the
source ---copied or fetched--- into the git-tracked =resources/redirects/<slug>.org=, and
includes that.

- *The property is never rewritten.* =:REDIRECT: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alhassy/holy-books/main/holy-books.org=
  stays as written, so every publish re-fetches the tip of the repo's default branch
  ---=master=, or =main= for younger repos; the URL names whichever the repo uses.
- *The vendored copy is a fallback, not a source.* When the source is unreachable we reuse
  last time's copy, with a message; only a source that is unreachable /and/ never vendored
  is an error.  An HTTP error counts as unreachable ---=url-copy-file= would happily vendor
  GitHub's =404: Not Found= page as our article!  So we fetch with =curl --fail=, which
  also sidesteps url.el's cache and its bodiless =304 Not Modified= replies.

Hence how far an article can lag its source:

| Source     | Laptop preview      | CI                         |
|------------+---------------------+----------------------------|
| URL        | fresh fetch         | fresh fetch                |
| local path | fresh copy          | the last committed copy    |

- *A URL article* lags its repo until the next blog build.  Pushes to the blog trigger
  builds, but pushes to =holy-books= don't; so CI also rebuilds daily ---see =schedule:= in
  §the CI workflow--- bounding the lag at a day.  =workflow_dispatch= is there for the
  impatient.
- *A local-path article* lags until we publish from the laptop and commit the refreshed
  copy; the diff of =resources/redirects/<slug>.org= is then a terse "what changed
  upstream" view.
- *A renamed branch or file* makes the fetch 404: we keep publishing the stale copy, and the
  message tells us to fix the =:REDIRECT:=.

*** Making a source include-ready
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: making-a-source-include-ready
:END:

Three things that are right in the repo are wrong in the blog, so =blog--prepare-redirect=
fixes them in the vendored copy:

1. *Relative links.* =[[file:images/short_example.png]]= is relative to the repo, but once
   vendored it'd resolve against =resources/redirects/=.  For a URL source we re-root it at
   the URL's directory, ↦
   =[[https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alhassy/holy-books/main/images/short_example.png]]=,
   which Org still inlines as an image.  A local-path source keeps its links; in due time,
   we may resolve them against the source's git remote.
2. *File-level keywords.* The temp file already opens with the heading's =#+title:= and
   friends; an included =#+title: holy-books= would be concatenated onto ours, and
   =#+options: toc:nil= would hide our Ξ table of contents.  So we axe =#+title:=,
   =#+subtitle:=, =#+author:=, =#+date:=, =#+options:=, and =#+export_file_name:=; and
   =#+toc:=, since Ξ already is one.
3. *=#+fileimage:=.* The repo declares its own article image; e.g.,
   =#+fileimage: images/short_example.png 350 253=, which 〔M-x my/blogging-mode〕 previews
   in the repo use too.  For a URL source we re-root its path like any relative link, and
   =blog--info-subtree= reads it from the vendored copy whenever the heading lacks an
   =:IMAGE:= of its own; so the index, tag pages, and RSS agree with the article.  Hence
   =blog-publish-all= vendors every redirect /before/ building the posts registry, lest
   CI's index show the image as of our last commit.

Lookalikes inside source blocks are left alone, since =org-element= only reports real
keywords and links.

A package's write-up is demonstrative of the package: =holy-books.org= is full of
=quran:1:1= and =basmala:= links, which only export once =holy-books.el= has registered them.
So a heading's =:LOAD:= names the package's =.el= ---a URL or a path, alongside its
=:REDIRECT:=--- which we vendor verbatim to =resources/redirects/<slug>.el=, with the same
fallback, and =load= before exporting.  CI thus needs no per-package install step, though
the package's own dependencies must already be among CI's packages.  (Loading fetched
code is trust: whoever can push to that repo can run code in our CI.  It's our repo.)

We skip the =load= when the package is already a feature, named after the =.el=;
e.g., =holy-books.el= ↦ =holy-books=.  CI starts afresh, so it always loads; but on our
laptop, previewing =projects.org= would otherwise clobber the working copy we're hacking
on with the GitHub tip ---whose bugs we may have just fixed, unpushed!

#+begin_src org :tangle no
,* Holy Books: Quran & Bible look-ups in Org-mode :emacs:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID:   holy-books-in-org-mode
:REDIRECT:    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alhassy/holy-books/main/holy-books.org
:LOAD:        https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alhassy/holy-books/main/holy-books.el
:END:

A single =.el= isn't always enough.  =org-special-block-extras.org= shows =osbe-example:box=,
an =org-defblock= in =tests/examples/README.el= that reads =tests/examples/box.el= beside it
and scans the repo's =*.el= for =box='s definition; fetched alone, it finds neither.  So a
=:LOAD:= that's neither a URL nor an absolute path is relative to the =:REDIRECT:='s GitHub
repo, which we shallow-clone to the git-ignored =.redirect-clones/<repo>/= ---refetched each
publish, with last time's clone as the offline fallback--- and load from there; e.g.,
=:LOAD: tests/examples/README.el=.  The directory is hidden, so it's neither scanned for
articles nor copied to the site, where a nested =.git= would break GitHub Pages.  One-off
link types thus stay with their article, rather than piling up in AlBasmala.org.

Finally, a redirected article on GitHub opens with a banner inviting readers to star the
repo or discuss it in its issues ---at the top, where it's seen, rather than after a long
read, where it's missed.  Its History badge points at the repo's log for the file, not at
=projects.org='s, and its Source badge colourises the vendored file, not the five-line
heading.

*** A REDIRECT to a container
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: a-redirect-to-a-container
:END:

=org-special-block-extras.org= is a 9,000-line manual; as one article, it buries the block
types under the pitch and the developer's guide under both.  So it declares
=#+article_style: multiple=, just like =projects.org=, and each public top-level heading
becomes its own article; its one =projects.org= heading still points at the whole file.

#+begin_src org :tangle no
,#+article_style: multiple
,* 🧙‍♂️ /Write Once, Export Everywhere/: Say Hello to =org-special-block-extras=
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID:   org-special-block-extras
:TITLE:       org-special-block-extras: Write Once, Export Everywhere
:DESCRIPTION: A unified interface for special blocks and links: defblock.
:END:
,* Block and link types provided by this package
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID:   org-special-block-extras-blocks-and-links
⋮

- *Registry.* =blog--info-multiple= swaps such a heading's info for its vendored source's
  article infos, via =blog--redirected-container-infos=; each inherits the heading's
  =:REDIRECT:=, =:LOAD:=, history, draft-ness, and tags, and remembers its slug as
  ="redirect-slug"=, which names the vendored file.  An =:ignore:= heading ---say, =HTML &
  LaTeX Setup=--- and the footnote section are never articles.
- *Body.* An article is =#+include: "vendored.org::#slug" :only-contents t :minlevel 1=,
  after =#+setupfile: vendored.org=, so the container's =#+macro:=s and =header-args=
  ---notably =:eval never-export=--- still apply.
- *Cross-article links.* =[[#Boxed-Text]]= in the pitch targets a section of another
  article, so =blog--prepare-redirect= rewrites it to
  =[[https://alhassy.com/org-special-block-extras-blocks-and-links#Boxed-Text][…]]=; a
  link within its own article stays put.

The first article reuses the heading's slug, so =alhassy.com/org-special-block-extras= still
lands on the pitch.

(defvar blog-redirects-subdir "resources/redirects/"
  "Subdirectory of `blog-posts-directory' where :REDIRECT: sources are
vendored for CI.  Relative path; must end with a trailing slash.")

(defun blog--url-p (path)
  "Return non-nil if PATH is an HTTP(S) URL."
  (string-match-p "\\`https?://" path))

(defun blog--github-raw-parts (redirect)

    (mapcar (lambda (i) (match-string i redirect)) '(1 2 3 4))))

  (pcase (blog--github-raw-parts redirect)
    (`(,owner ,repo ,branch ,path) (format "https://github.com/%s/%s/commits/%s/%s" owner repo branch path))))

(defun blog--fetch-url (url file)
  "Write URL's body to FILE; an HTTP error is an error, lest we vendor a “404: Not Found” page."
  ;; Not url.el: its cache sends If-Modified-Since, and a bodiless 304 isn't an article.
  (unless (zerop (call-process "curl" nil nil nil "--fail" "--silent" "--show-error" "--location"
                               "--max-time" "30" "--output" file url))
    (error "curl couldn't fetch %s" url)))

(defconst blog--redirect-axed-keywords '("TITLE" "SUBTITLE" "AUTHOR" "DATE" "OPTIONS" "EXPORT_FILE_NAME" "TOC")
  "A :REDIRECT: source's file-level keywords that would clash with its article's synthetic ones.")

(defun blog--prepare-redirect (file redirect)
  "Make FILE, vendored from REDIRECT, include-ready; e.g., when REDIRECT is a URL, file:images/x.png ↦ URL-dir/images/x.png.

Axes `blog--redirect-axed-keywords', re-roots relative file links and #+fileimage: at REDIRECT's
directory when it's a URL, and, in a container, points [[#id]] at the article holding id when
that's another one; see §“Making a source include-ready”."
  (with-temp-buffer
    (insert-file-contents file)
    (delay-mode-hooks (org-mode))
    (let* ((base    (and (blog--url-p redirect) (file-name-directory redirect)))
           (tree    (org-element-parse-buffer))
           ;; The :CUSTOM_ID: of EL's top-level heading, i.e., its article's slug in a container.
           (article (lambda (el) (when-let ((top (car (last (seq-filter (lambda (e) (eq 'headline (org-element-type e)))
                                                                         (org-element-lineage el))))))
                                   (org-element-property :CUSTOM_ID top))))
           ;; id ↦ its heading's article, when this source is a container; e.g., "Boxed-Text" ↦ "org-special-block-extras-blocks-and-links".
           (homes   (when (blog--multiple-style-p file)
                      (org-element-map tree 'headline
                        (lambda (h) (when-let ((id (org-element-property :CUSTOM_ID h)))
                                      (cons id (if (= 1 (org-element-property :level h)) id (funcall article h)))))))))
      ;; Edit back to front, so the positions of earlier elements stay valid.
      (dolist (el (nreverse (org-element-map tree '(keyword link) #'identity)))
        (let ((beg  (org-element-property :begin el))
              (end  (org-element-property :end el))
              (type (org-element-property :type el))
              (path (org-element-property :path el)))
          (cond
           ((member (org-element-property :key el) blog--redirect-axed-keywords)
            (delete-region beg end))
           ((and base (equal (org-element-property :key el) "FILEIMAGE"))
            (let* ((image (car (split-string (org-element-property :value el))))
                   ;; Expand before searching: `url-expand-file-name' clobbers the match data.
                   (url   (and image (not (blog--url-p image)) (not (file-name-absolute-p image))
                               (url-expand-file-name image base))))
              (when url
                (goto-char beg)
                (search-forward image end)
                (replace-match url t t))))
           ((and (equal type "custom-id") (map-elt homes path)
                 (not (equal (map-elt homes path) (funcall article el))))
            ;; Another article's section: link to that article, keeping our description or using its id.
            (let ((desc (if (org-element-property :contents-begin el)
                            (buffer-substring (org-element-property :contents-begin el) (org-element-property :contents-end el))
                          path)))
              (delete-region beg (- end (org-element-property :post-blank el)))
              (goto-char beg)
              (insert (format "[[%s#%s][%s]]" (blog--url (map-elt homes path)) path desc))))
           ((and base (equal type "file") (not (file-name-absolute-p path)))
            ;; Expand before searching: `url-expand-file-name' clobbers the match data.
            (let ((url (url-expand-file-name path base)))
              (goto-char beg)
              (search-forward (org-element-property :raw-link el) end)
              (replace-match url t t)))))))
    (write-region nil nil file nil 'silent)))

(defun blog--vendor-one-redirect (slug redirect &optional ext)
  "(SLUG, REDIRECT, EXT) ↦ `blog-redirects-subdir'/SLUG.EXT, freshly copied or fetched from REDIRECT.

EXT defaults to \".org\", and only then is the copy made include-ready; a :LOAD:'s \".el\" is
vendored verbatim.  When REDIRECT is unreachable ---no network, or CI lacking ~/.emacs.d/--- we
keep last time's copy, and only signal an error when there is none."
  (let* ((vendored (concat blog-redirects-subdir slug (or ext ".org")))
         (dest     (expand-file-name vendored blog-posts-directory))
         (tmp      (make-temp-file "albasmala-redirect-" nil ".org")))
    (make-directory (file-name-directory dest) t)
    (condition-case err
        (progn
          (if (blog--url-p redirect)
              (blog--fetch-url redirect tmp)
            (copy-file (expand-file-name redirect) tmp t))
          (unless ext (blog--prepare-redirect tmp redirect))
          (rename-file tmp dest t)
          ;; From the blog's root: our caller may be a temp buffer, outside the repo.
          (let ((default-directory (expand-file-name blog-posts-directory)))
            (call-process "git" nil nil nil "add" dest)))
      (error
       (when (file-exists-p tmp) (delete-file tmp))
       (unless (file-exists-p dest)
         (user-error "REDIRECT %s is unreachable and was never vendored: %s" redirect (error-message-string err)))
       (message "=> REDIRECT %s is unreachable (%s); reusing the vendored %s"
                redirect (error-message-string err) vendored)))
    vendored))

(defun blog--clone-redirect-repo (redirect)
  "REDIRECT ↦ the directory of a fresh shallow clone of its GitHub repo, .redirect-clones/REPO/.

When the fetch fails ---say, no network--- we keep last time's clone, and only signal an error when there is none."
  (pcase-let* ((`(,owner ,repo ,branch ,_) (or (blog--github-raw-parts redirect)
                                               (user-error "A repo-relative :LOAD: needs a GitHub :REDIRECT:, not %s" redirect)))
               (dir (file-name-as-directory (expand-file-name (concat ".redirect-clones/" repo) blog-posts-directory)))
               (url (format "https://github.com/%s/%s.git" owner repo))
               (git (lambda (&rest args) (zerop (apply #'call-process "git" nil nil nil args)))))
    (cond
     ((not (file-directory-p dir))
      (make-directory (file-name-directory (directory-file-name dir)) t)
      (unless (funcall git "clone" "--quiet" "--depth" "1" "--branch" branch url dir)
        (user-error "Couldn't clone %s, and there's no earlier clone at %s" url dir)))
     ((not (and (funcall git "-C" dir "fetch" "--quiet" "--depth" "1" "origin" branch)
                (funcall git "-C" dir "reset" "--quiet" "--hard" "FETCH_HEAD")))
      (message "=> Couldn't fetch %s; reusing the clone at %s" url dir)))
    dir))

(defun blog--redirect-load-file (slug redirect lib)
  "(SLUG, REDIRECT, LIB) ↦ the file to `load' for :LOAD: LIB; a URL or absolute path is vendored, else LIB is relative to REDIRECT's repo."
  (if (or (blog--url-p lib) (file-name-absolute-p lib))
      (expand-file-name (blog--vendor-one-redirect slug lib ".el") blog-posts-directory)
    (expand-file-name lib (blog--clone-redirect-repo redirect))))

(defun blog--map-publishable-top-headings (fn)
  "Walk top-level headings in the current buffer and call FN with point on each.

Only runs on multiple-style containers.  Skips COMMENTed headings ---the
per-heading FN is responsible for any further filtering (e.g. :noexport:)."
  (when (blog--multiple-style-p)
    (save-excursion
      (goto-char (point-min))
      (while (re-search-forward "^\\* " nil t)
        (unless (org-element-property :commentedp (org-element-at-point))
          (funcall fn))
        (org-end-of-subtree t t)))))

(defun blog--assign-slugs ()
  "Walk the current buffer's top-level headings; for each without a :CUSTOM_ID:
property, derive one from the heading title (or :TITLE: override) and
write it back via `org-entry-put'.

Called from the C-x C-s preview binding so that, by the time a source
lands in git, every container heading carries a stable :CUSTOM_ID:.  CI then
reads :CUSTOM_ID: and errors out if missing, rather than silently generating
one and writing back to a throwaway checkout."
  (blog--map-publishable-top-headings
   (lambda ()
     (let ((tags (mapcar #'downcase (org-get-tags))))

                   (org-entry-get (point) "CUSTOM_ID"))
         (let* ((title (org-get-heading t t t t))
                (slug  (blog--make-slug
                        (or (org-entry-get (point) "TITLE") title))))
           (org-entry-put (point) "CUSTOM_ID" slug)
           (message "=> Assigned :CUSTOM_ID: %s to %s" slug title)))))))

(defun blog--bump-modified-stamp ()
  "Update the MODIFIED timestamp in the current buffer on every C-x C-s.

For standalone-style buffers: rewrites the #+modified: file keyword if
present, or inserts it after #+date: (creating #+date: too if absent).
For multiple-style buffers: sets the :MODIFIED: property on the
top-level heading at point if the property is already present, otherwise
leaves the heading untouched ---absence is intentional (silent edits)."
  (let ((today (format-time-string "%Y-%m-%d")))
    (if (blog--multiple-style-p)
        ;; Multiple style: update :MODIFIED: on the enclosing top-level heading.
        (save-excursion
          (org-back-to-heading t)
          (when (org-entry-get (point) "MODIFIED")
            (org-entry-put (point) "MODIFIED" today)))
      ;; Standalone style: update or insert #+modified: file keyword.
      (save-excursion      
      (save-restriction
        (widen)
        (goto-char (point-min))
        (if (re-search-forward "^#\\+modified:[ ]*.*$" nil t)
            (replace-match (concat "#+modified: " today))
          ;; Insert after #+date: if present, else after the last #+keyword: block.
          (goto-char (point-min))
          (if (re-search-forward "^#\\+date:.*$" nil t)
              (end-of-line)
            (re-search-forward "^#\\+[a-zA-Z].*$" nil t)
            (while (looking-at "\n#\\+[a-zA-Z]")
              (forward-line 1)
              (end-of-line)))
          (insert "\n#+modified: " today)))))))

(defun blog--show-preview (url)
  "Display URL in an xwidget buffer to the right of the current Org window.

Guarantees [Org source | xwidget] side-by-side layout:
- If there is already an xwidget window on the right, reuse it.
- Otherwise split the current window to the right and open there.
- The Org source window is never taken over."
  (delete-other-windows)  
  (let* ((org-win   (selected-window))
         (xw-buf    (car (blog--xwidget-buffers)))

          (select-window xw-win)
          (xwidget-webkit-browse-url url)
          (select-window org-win))

(defun blog--preview-subtree ()
  "Preview the top-level heading at point as a standalone blog article.

For use in multiple-style (#+article_style: multiple) files.
Called by C-x C-s under `my/blogging-mode'.

Opens (or reuses) an xwidget window to the right of the Org source buffer,
maintaining a stable [Org source | xwidget] side-by-side layout."
  (let ((kill-buffer-query-functions nil))
    (unless (blog--multiple-style-p)
      (user-error "Not a multiple-style file"))

      (unless (org-at-heading-p) (outline-previous-heading))
      (while (> (org-outline-level) 1) (org-up-heading-safe))

                            (blog--make-slug (or (org-entry-get (point) "TITLE") title))))

        (blog--publish-single-subtree (point) (buffer-file-name) info)

          ;; blog--show-preview reuses the existing xwidget window when present,
          ;; so the [Org | xwidget] split is stable across repeated saves.

(defun blog--draft-file-p (file)

(defun blog--info (post-filename)
  "Extract the `#+BLOG_KEYWORD: VALUE` pairs from POST-FILENAME.

Example use: (blog--info \"~/blog/HeytingAlgebra.org\")

For container sub-articles, the temp file may carry synthetic keywords:
  #+history_url:         ---overrides the auto-computed GitHub history URL
  #+htmlized_source_url: ---URL for the per-article colourised source badge
These are ignored for ordinary standalone files (regex yields nil, fallback applies)."
  (let ((case-fold-search t))
    (with-temp-buffer
      (insert-file-contents post-filename)
      (let* ((keyword-pairs
              (cl-loop for (prop.name prop.regex prop.default) on
                    `("title"                "^\\#\\+title:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"                ,post-filename
                      "date"                 "^\\#\\+modified:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"             ,(format-time-string "%Y-%m-%d")
                      "image"                "^\\#\\+fileimage: \\(.*\\)"                "emacs-birthday-present.png 350 350"

                      "history_url"          "^\\#\\+history_url:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"          nil
                      "htmlized_source_url"  "^\\#\\+htmlized_source_url:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"  nil
                      "site_nav"             "^\\#\\+site_nav:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"             nil
                      "modified"             "^\\#\\+modified:[ ]*\\(.+\\)$"             nil
                      )
                  by 'cdddr
                  ;; See: https://stackoverflow.com/questions/19774603/convert-alist-to-from-regular-list-in-elisp
                  do (goto-char (point-min))
                  collect (cons prop.name
                                (if (search-forward-regexp prop.regex nil t)
                                    (match-string 1)
                                  prop.default)))))
        (-snoc
         (cons
          (cons "file" (f-base post-filename))
          keyword-pairs)
         (cons "url" (blog--url (f-base post-filename)))
         ;; A draft says #+draft: t, or lives under draft/; the temp file of a container draft says the former.
         (cons "draft" (when (or (blog--draft-file-p post-filename)

** blog--info-subtree, blog--info-multiple: Extract metadata from container headings :details:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: blog-info-subtree-blog-info-multiple-extract-metadata-from-container-headings
:END:

The =multiple=-style analogues of =blog--info=: =blog--info-subtree= reads a heading's
drawer and tags via =org-element=.  =blog--info-multiple= parses the container /once/
---per-heading parses would be quadratic--- and yields one alist per publishable heading,
with =blog--info='s keys plus ="slug"= and ="container"=.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(defun blog--info-subtree-abstract (headline-node)
  "Return the abstract text for HEADLINE-NODE, or nil.

Priority:
1. :ABSTRACT: property on the headline.
2. A child heading whose title matches /abstract/i (e.g. ** Abstract :ignore:).
3. The first paragraph element in the heading body."
  (or
   ;; 1. Explicit property
   (org-element-property :ABSTRACT headline-node)
   ;; 2. Child heading named Abstract
   (org-element-map headline-node 'headline
     (lambda (child)
       (when (and (= (org-element-property :level child)
                    (1+ (org-element-property :level headline-node)))
                  (string-match-p "abstract"
                                  (downcase (or (org-element-property :raw-value child) ""))))
         (org-element-interpret-data (org-element-contents child))))
     nil t)
   ;; 3. First paragraph fallback
   (org-element-map headline-node 'paragraph
     (lambda (para) (org-element-interpret-data para))
     nil t)))

(defun blog--info-subtree (headline-node container-file slug)
  "Extract post metadata for HEADLINE-NODE from CONTAINER-FILE with SLUG.

Returns nil when the headline carries a :noexport: or :ignore: tag, the COMMENT keyword,
or is the footnote section: none of these is an article.
Returns an alist with the same keys as blog--info plus \"slug\" and \"container\"."
  (let* ((heading-tags (mapcar #'downcase
                               (org-element-property :tags headline-node))))
    (unless (or (member "noexport" heading-tags)
                (member "ignore" heading-tags)
                (org-element-property :commentedp headline-node)
                (org-element-property :footnote-section-p headline-node))
      (let* (;; draft?
             (draft? (or (member "draft" heading-tags)
                         (equal "t" (org-element-property :DRAFT headline-node))
                         (blog--draft-file-p container-file)))
             ;; title: :TITLE: property overrides heading text
             (title (or (org-element-property :TITLE headline-node)
                        (org-element-property :raw-value headline-node)))
             ;; date ---prefer :MODIFIED:, fall back to :DATE: for old articles
             (date-raw (or (org-element-property :MODIFIED headline-node)
                           (org-element-property :DATE headline-node)))
             (date (if date-raw
                       (replace-regexp-in-string "[<>]" "" date-raw)
                     (format-time-string "%Y-%m-%d")))
             ;; description
             (description (or (org-element-property :DESCRIPTION headline-node)
                              "I learned something neat, and wanted to share!"))
             ;; image: explicit :IMAGE: property wins; then a :REDIRECT: source's own #+fileimage:; then tags
             (image (or (org-element-property :IMAGE headline-node)
                        (and (org-element-property :REDIRECT headline-node)
                             (blog--redirect-keyword slug "fileimage"))
                        (blog--image-for-tags heading-tags)))
             ;; tags: merge :TAGS: property with heading tags; strip structural tags
             (structural-tags '("noexport" "draft" "ignore" "details" "details_orange"
                                "details_green" "header" "reexport" "noreexport"))
             (prop-tags  (s-split " " (or (org-element-property :TAGS headline-node) "") t))
             (org-tags   (seq-remove (lambda (tag) (member tag structural-tags)) heading-tags))
             (all-tags   (seq-uniq (append prop-tags org-tags)))
             (tags-str   (s-join " " all-tags))
             ;; abstract
             (abstract (blog--info-subtree-abstract headline-node))
             ;; urls
             (container-base (f-base container-file))
             (url      (blog--url slug))
             (redirect (org-element-property :REDIRECT headline-node))
             ;; A redirected article's history is its source's, when that's on GitHub.
             (history  (or (and redirect (blog--redirect-history-url redirect))
                           (blog--history-url container-file))))
        (list (cons "file"        container-base)
              (cons "slug"        slug)
              (cons "container"   container-base)
              (cons "title"       title)
              (cons "date"        date)
              (cons "image"       image)
              (cons "description" description)
              (cons "tags"        tags-str)
              (cons "url"         url)
              (cons "history"     history)
              (cons "abstract"    abstract)
              (cons "draft"       (if draft? "t" nil))
              (cons "redirect"    redirect)
              (cons "load"        (org-element-property :LOAD headline-node))
              (cons "modified"    (org-element-property :MODIFIED headline-node))
              (cons "site_nav"    (org-element-property :SITE_NAV headline-node)))))))

(defun blog--info-multiple (container-file)
  "Return a list of post-alists for all publishable top-level headings in CONTAINER-FILE.

CONTAINER-FILE must carry #+article_style: multiple.
Headings tagged :noexport: or carrying the COMMENT keyword are excluded.
Headings tagged :draft: are included but marked."
  (with-temp-buffer
    (insert-file-contents container-file)
    (org-mode)
    (let* ((tree (org-element-parse-buffer))
           ;; Collect all level-1 headings
           (top-headings
            (org-element-map tree 'headline
              (lambda (h)
                (when (= (org-element-property :level h) 1) h))))
           ;; A slug is the heading's :CUSTOM_ID:.  Until C-x C-s stamps one, fall back to
           ;; what it will stamp; `blog--validate-slugs' catches any collision.
           (slugs
            (mapcar (lambda (h)
                      (or (org-element-property :CUSTOM_ID h)
                          (blog--make-slug (or (org-element-property :TITLE h)
                                               (org-element-property :raw-value h)))))
                    top-headings)))
      ;; Build alists, skipping noexport/COMMENT headings (blog--info-subtree returns nil for them);
      ;; a :REDIRECT: to a container becomes that container's articles.
      (mapcan #'blog--redirected-container-infos
              (delq nil
                    (cl-mapcar (lambda (h slug) (blog--info-subtree h container-file slug))
                               top-headings slugs))))))

(defun blog--redirected-container-infos (info)
  "INFO ↦ (INFO), or the infos of its vendored :REDIRECT: source's articles when that's a container.

Each inherits INFO's redirect, load, history, draft, and tags, and remembers INFO's slug as
\"redirect-slug\", which names the vendored file; see §“A REDIRECT to a container”."
  (let ((vendored (and (map-elt info "redirect")
                       (expand-file-name (concat blog-redirects-subdir (@slug info) ".org") blog-posts-directory))))
    (if (not (and vendored (file-exists-p vendored) (blog--multiple-style-p vendored)))
        (list info)
      (mapcar (lambda (article)
                (append (list (cons "redirect-slug" (@slug info))
                              (cons "tags" (s-join " " (seq-uniq (s-split " " (concat (map-elt info "tags") " " (map-elt article "tags")) t)))))
                        (map-filter (lambda (key _) (member key '("redirect" "load" "history" "draft"))) info)
                        article))
              (blog--info-multiple vendored)))))

** blog-posts, blog-tags, and their consumers: tag & index page generation :details:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: blog-posts-blog-tags-and-their-consumers-tag-index-page-generation
:END:

- =blog-posts= ---every post-alist from the =.org= files under =~/blog/=, newest first; =blog-make-index-page=
  turns each into a card on the index and tag pages.
- =blog-tags= ---the sorted, unique tags of =blog--listed-posts=, which omits §drafts; for tag
  pages and completion.  Listings ---the index, tag pages, and RSS--- draw on =blog--listed-posts= too.

=(blog--refresh-posts)= sets both whenever =my/blogging-mode= starts, on every save, and atop every publish.

Every post-alist enters through =blog--file-infos=, so that is where we check it.  =date-to-time=
never complains: =#+modified: Fri= silently becomes 1970, and =2023-13-45= some other day, misplacing
the article on the index and in the RSS feed.  Hence an =lf-assert= that each date names a real
calendar day; on failure it shows the source file, title, and offending date at once.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp  -n
(defun blog--date-p (str)
  "STR ↦ non-nil iff it names a real calendar day; e.g., “2026-05-04 Mon” ↦ t, “Fri” ↦ nil, “2023-02-30” ↦ nil."
  (pcase-let ((`(,_ ,_ ,_ ,day ,month ,year) (parse-time-string (or str ""))))
    (and day month year (<= 1 month 12) (<= 1 day (date-days-in-month year month)))))

(defvar blog--file-infos-cache (make-hash-table :test #'equal)
  "FILE ↦ (MTIME . INFOS): what `blog--file-infos' last computed for FILE.")

(defun blog--file-infos (file)
  "FILE ↦ its post infos (nil when unpublishable), recomputed only when FILE's mtime moves.

C-x C-s refreshes the registry on every save; with the cache that costs one
`file-attributes' per file, re-reading only the files that actually changed."
  (let ((mtime (file-attribute-modification-time (file-attributes file)))
        (hit (gethash file blog--file-infos-cache)))
    (if (and hit (equal mtime (car hit)))
        (cdr hit)
      (let ((infos (when (blog--publishable-p file)
                     ;; Each info remembers its source's path, e.g., "emacs/foo.org", for error messages.
                     (let ((source (file-relative-name file (expand-file-name blog-posts-directory))))
                       (mapcar (lambda (info) (cons (cons "source" source) info))
                               (if (blog--multiple-style-p file)
                                   (blog--info-multiple file)
                                 (list (blog--info file))))))))
        (dolist (info infos)
          (lf-assert (map-elt info "source") t
                     (@title info)           t
                     (@date info)            (blog--date-p it)))
        (puthash file (cons mtime infos) blog--file-infos-cache)
        infos))))

(defun blog--compute-posts-and-pages ()
  "Scan every .org file under `blog-posts-directory' and return (posts . pages).

Every .org file is processed uniformly ---there is no special-cased directory.
Container files (#+article_style: multiple) yield many post entries; standalone
files yield one.  A file with #+site_nav: contributes a nav-page entry instead
of (or in addition to) a post entry.

posts ---all entries, sorted newest-first.
pages ---site_nav entries, unsorted (used for the nav bar)."
  (let ((posts '())
        (pages '()))
    (dolist (file (blog--org-files))
      (dolist (info (blog--file-infos file))
        (when (map-elt info "site_nav") (push info pages))
        (push info posts)))
    (cons (sort posts (lambda (a b)
                        (time-less-p (date-to-time (@date b))
                                     (date-to-time (@date a)))))
          pages)))

(defun blog--validate-slugs ()
  "Signal a `user-error' when a container slug isn't lower-case kebab-case, or two articles share a slug.

Scans blog-posts, which covers both standalone and container articles.  The
effective slug is the :CUSTOM_ID: (container subtrees) or the file basename
(standalone articles); both land at the same URL, so both must be globally
unique.  Only :CUSTOM_ID: slugs must be kebab-case: a standalone basename
like =PathCat= is a long-published URL."
  (let ((seen (make-hash-table :test #'equal)))
    (dolist (p blog-posts)
      (let* ((slug   (or (@slug p) (@file p)))  ; effective URL slug
             (source (format "\"%s\" (%s)"
                             (or (map-elt p "title") slug)
                             (or (map-elt p "source") slug)))
             (prior  (gethash slug seen)))
        (when (and (@slug p) (not (equal slug (blog--make-slug slug))))
          (user-error "Slug \"%s\" of %s isn't lower-case kebab-case; use :CUSTOM_ID: %s"
                      slug source (blog--make-slug slug)))
        (when prior
          ;; Subdirectories don't reach the flat site: emacs/foo.org and life/foo.org both claim foo.html.
          (user-error "Duplicate slug \"%s\":\n  already claimed by %s\n  also claimed by %s\n  %s"
                      slug prior source
                      (if (@slug p) "Change one of the :CUSTOM_ID: properties."
                        "Rename one of the files; subdirectories don't count, the site is flat.")))
        (puthash slug source seen)))))

(defun blog--link (link desc info)
  "Org's HTML for LINK, a relative file path re-rooted at `blog-posts-directory'; the site is flat.

By way of example, in =emacs/foo.org=, =[[file:../resources/x.png]]= ↦ =resources/x.png= and
=[[file:../life/bar.org]]= ↦ =bar.html=, since every page is served from the root.  Files outside

  (let ((root (file-name-as-directory (expand-file-name blog-posts-directory)))
        (base (or blog--link-base-directory
                  (file-name-directory (or (plist-get info :input-file) default-directory))))
        (path (org-element-property :path link)))
    (when (and (equal (org-element-property :type link) "file")
               (not (file-name-absolute-p path))
               (file-in-directory-p base root))
      (let ((target (expand-file-name path base)))
        ;; Another article is a sibling on the flat site, wherever its source lives.
        (org-element-put-property link :path (if (equal (file-name-extension target) "org")
                                                 (file-name-nondirectory target)
                                               (file-relative-name target root)))))
    ;; Org's own radio links use a random id, while their <<<target>>> uses its label; agree on the label.
    (if-let ((radio (and (equal (org-element-property :type link) "radio")
                         (org-export-resolve-radio-link link info))))
        (format "<a href=\"#%s\">%s</a>" (org-html--reference radio info) desc)
      (org-html-link link desc info))))

(defun blog--stable-headline-ids (tree _backend info)
  "Give each headline lacking a :CUSTOM_ID: the slug `blog--ensure-useful-section-anchors' would stamp.

That function only runs on C-x C-s; here we cover files not re-saved since, so
CI never falls back to Org's random `orgXXXXXXX' anchors.  Collisions get -2, -3, …"
  (let* ((headlines (org-element-map tree 'headline #'identity info))
         (taken (delq nil (mapcar (lambda (h) (org-element-property :CUSTOM_ID h)) headlines))))
    (dolist (h headlines tree)
      (unless (org-element-property :CUSTOM_ID h)
        (let* ((base (blog--make-slug (org-element-property :raw-value h)))
               (base (if (string-empty-p base) "section" base))
               (slug base)
               (n 1))
          (while (member slug taken) (setq slug (format "%s-%d" base (cl-incf n))))
          (push slug taken)
          (org-element-put-property h :CUSTOM_ID slug))))))

  "HTML ↦ HTML sans Org's random `orgXXXXXXX' ids; signal, with a suggested name, when a link targets one.

  "Tag the “* Abstract” heading :abstract:, for =blog= exports only; e.g., =* Abstract :ignore:= ↦ =* Abstract :ignore:abstract:=.

** Colourised source: one consistent theme
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: colourised-source-one-consistent-theme
:END:

A colourised =.org.html= page is only as colourful as the faces Emacs has when it is made.  CI runs
Emacs in batch, with no theme, so we load =tsdh-light= ---and =org-modern=, when available--- to give
every source view, and every exported =#+begin_src= block, the same look we get in-buffer.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
;; Load a colour theme so htmlize has real colours (not just bold/italic)
;; to bake into the `#+begin_src' blocks of every exported article, and
;; into the per-article `.org.html' colourised-source views.
;;
;; In batch mode no theme is active by default ---font-lock faces then
;; carry only weight/slant attributes, which is why Elisp blocks looked
;; monochrome on deployed AlBasmala.html.  Most built-in themes
;; (`tango', `leuven', `wombat', …) leave `font-lock-keyword-face' et al
;; with `:foreground unspecified', so loading them is a no-op for
;; htmlize.  `tsdh-light' is the exception among shipped themes: it
;; assigns explicit colours to the full font-lock face family ---which is
;; exactly what we want baked into the HTML via `inline-css'.
(when noninteractive
  (load-theme 'tsdh-light t)
  ;; org-special-block-extras stores documentation strings as tooltips
  ;; using `font-lock-comment-face'; make sure the theme has taken effect
  ;; before any export buffer is created.
  (global-font-lock-mode 1)
  ;; Fontify headings, TODO/DONE keywords, tags, priorities the org-modern
  ;; way ---htmlize's `inline-css' mode bakes those face colours into every
  ;; .org.html page, so the colourised-source view inherits the same look
  ;; we get in-buffer.  Fancy glyph substitutions (prettified bullets) are
  ;; `display' overlays and don't survive htmlize ---that's fine; the
  ;; colour wins carry most of the aesthetic.
  (when (require 'org-modern nil t)
    (global-org-modern-mode 1)))
#+end_src

*** Ensuring Useful HTML Anchors
:PROPERTIES:

Upon HTML export, each tree heading is assigned an ID to be used for hyperlinks.
Default IDs are something like ~org1957a9d~, which does not endure the test of time:
Re-export will produce a different id. Hence, on every save, we give each heading
lacking a =CUSTOM_ID= its kebab-case slug; files not re-saved since get the same slug
at export time, via =blog--stable-headline-ids=, so CI never emits random ids.

Two headings named, say, =Notes= would share the slug =notes=; plain 〔C-x C-s〕 refuses, while
〔C-u C-x C-s〕 stamps =notes--placeholder-2=, … so we can preview a rough draft right away.  Since
=blog--make-slug= collapses dash runs, no real slug contains =--=, so the marker is unambiguous:
every later save logs each placeholder to =*Messages*= ---〔C-h e---〕 until we name it properly.

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(defconst blog--placeholder-id-regexp "--placeholder-[0-9]+\\'"
  "Matches a placeholder CUSTOM_ID; `blog--make-slug' collapses dash runs, so no real slug contains “--”.")

(defun blog--ensure-useful-section-anchors (&optional placeholders)
  "Org sections without a CUSTOM_ID are given one derived from the heading title.

Uses `blog--make-slug' so the result is lowercase kebab-case, consistent with
URL slugs.  On a collision, pops a message-box and undoes ---unless PLACEHOLDERS
(C-u C-x C-s), whence the id becomes a unique placeholder to tidy up in due time.

E.g., ↯ We'll go on a ∀∃⇅ adventure
   ↦  well-go-on-a-adventure
   ↦  well-go-on-a-adventure--placeholder-2, when repeated with PLACEHOLDERS"
  (interactive "P")
  ;; Existing ids count too, else a fresh slug could silently clash with a hand-written one.
  (let ((ids (org-map-entries (lambda () (org-entry-get nil "CUSTOM_ID")) "CUSTOM_ID={.}")))
    (org-map-entries
     (lambda ()
       (org-with-point-at (point)
         (unless (org-entry-get nil "CUSTOM_ID")
           (let* ((slug (blog--make-slug (nth 4 (org-heading-components))))
                  (id slug)
                  (n 1))
             (when (member id ids)
               (unless placeholders
                 (message-box "Oh no, a repeated id!\n\n\t%s\n\nC-u C-x C-s makes every repeated id unique, e.g., %s--placeholder-2, so we can preview now and name them in due time."
                              id id)
                 (undo)
                 (setq quit-flag t))
               (while (member id ids)
                 (setq id (format "%s--placeholder-%d" slug (cl-incf n)))))
             (push id ids)
             (org-entry-put nil "CUSTOM_ID" id))))))))

(defun blog--warn-placeholder-ids ()
  "Log each placeholder CUSTOM_ID, with its heading, to *Messages*; then say how many there are."

                    "CUSTOM_ID={--placeholder-[0-9]+$}")))
    (dolist (h hits) (message "%s" h))
    (message "⚠ %d placeholder id(s) in this article; C-h e to see them all" (length hits))))

** RSS feeds: one per tag, plus a global one                       :details:
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: rss-feeds-one-per-tag-plus-a-global-one
:END:

RSS, /Really Simple Syndication/, is a plain-text format a website uses to announce its
new content.  A reader program ---Feedly, Inoreader, Newsboat, NetNewsWire, and dozens of
others--- subscribes to the URL of a feed and periodically refetches it; when the feed has
a new =<item>=, the reader surfaces it as "there is a new article".  The blog does not
push; readers /pull/ whenever they want.

So every time we publish, this pipeline regenerates =rss.xml=; the next time a reader's
program polls our URL, it compares entries against what it saw last time and tells the
human about any new ones.  We never send email, never track subscribers, never even know
who is subscribed.  Publishing is just "put a new item in the feed"; the rest is the
reader's problem.

Why bother at all?  Because RSS is the one remaining ambient-notification mechanism for
writers on the open web: no algorithm, no platform lock-in, no "follow" button owned by a
company.  A reader who subscribed in 2008 is still subscribed today, so long as the URL
resolves.  For a personal technical blog, it is the cheapest way to have an audience that
returns.

We emit two flavours of feed:
- =rss.xml= ---every post, newest first, for the reader who wants everything we write.
- =<tag>-rss.xml= ---one per tag, e.g., =emacs-rss.xml= or =arabic-rss.xml=, for the
  reader who wants only those articles; each is linked atop its tag page.

Each item carries a =<pubDate>=, a =<link>= back to =alhassy.com/<slug>=, and the
article's =#+description= as its preview text.  Bodies are intentionally not embedded: we
want readers to click through.

*On identity and what counts as “new”.*  Every =<item>= also carries a =<guid>=, a
/globally unique identifier/.  Feed readers remember which guids they have already shown
their user, and on every refetch compare the feed's current guids against that seen-set:
- /New guid/ ↦ the reader announces it as a new post.
- /Already-seen guid/ ↦ the reader ignores the item, /even if its title, description, or
  pubDate changed/.

Hence the guid, not the content, decides what our readers hear about.  Ours is

: alhassy.com/<slug>?last-updated=<MODIFIED>

or just =alhassy.com/<slug>= without a =:MODIFIED:= (=#+modified:= for standalone posts).
Hence an edit re-announces an article exactly when its =:MODIFIED:= changes.

*Partial-commit trick.*  =:MODIFIED:= is bumped on every save, and Magit decides the rest:
stage its hunk with =s= to announce the edit, or unstage it with =u= to ship a typo fix
quietly.  The “should this notify?” decision lives at commit time, alongside the change.
Deriving the date from =git log= instead would make every =[typo]= commit re-announce.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp  -n
(defun blog--rss-date (date-str)
  "Format DATE-STR (an Org date string) as an RFC-822 pubDate for RSS."
  (format-time-string "%a, %d %b %Y %H:%M:%S %z"
                      (condition-case _
                          (date-to-time date-str)
                        (error (current-time)))))

(defun blog--rss-guid (post)
  "Return POST's RSS <guid>.

If POST carries a :MODIFIED: property, the guid is the article URL with
a ?last-updated=<MODIFIED> query string; bumping :MODIFIED: therefore
re-announces the post to every subscriber's feed reader (see the
identity discussion in the prose above).  Without :MODIFIED:, the guid
is just the URL ---edits stay quiet until you stamp one."
  (let ((modified (map-elt post "modified")))
    (if modified
        (format "%s?last-updated=%s" (@url post) modified)
      (@url post))))

(defun blog--make-one-rss-feed (posts filename &optional channel-title)
  "Emit `blog-publish-directory'/FILENAME from POSTS.

A minimal RSS 2.0 feed ---title, link, pubDate, description per item.
Body is intentionally a short #+description rather than the full article
HTML, so readers click through to alhassy.com.  CHANNEL-TITLE defaults
to `blog-title'.

Text fields are run through `xml-escape-string' so <, >, & in titles
and descriptions do not break feed readers."
  (cl-flet ((esc (s) (xml-escape-string (or s ""))))
    (let ((dest  (concat blog-publish-directory filename))
          (title (or channel-title blog-title)))
      (with-temp-file dest
        (insert "<?xml version=\"1.0\" encoding=\"UTF-8\"?>\n"
                "<rss version=\"2.0\">\n"
                "<channel>\n"
                "<title>"       (esc title) "</title>\n"
                "<link>"        (esc blog-url) "</link>\n"
                "<description>" (esc title) "</description>\n"
                "<language>en-us</language>\n")
        (dolist (post posts)
          (insert "<item>\n"
                  "<title>"       (esc (@title post))       "</title>\n"
                  "<link>"        (esc (@url post))         "</link>\n"
                  "<guid>"        (esc (blog--rss-guid post)) "</guid>\n"
                  "<pubDate>"     (blog--rss-date (@date post)) "</pubDate>\n"
                  "<description>" (esc (@description post)) "</description>\n"
                  "</item>\n"))
        (insert "</channel>\n</rss>\n")))))

(defun blog--posts-by-tag ()
  "Return a hash-table mapping tag-string → list of posts carrying that tag.

Each post appears under every tag it declares in #+filetags.  Lookup is
O(1); the table mirrors `blog-posts' and should be rebuilt after every
`blog--refresh-posts' call ---cheapest to just call this fresh at each
consumer (index/RSS loops)."
  (let ((by-tag (make-hash-table :test #'equal)))
    (dolist (p (blog--listed-posts))
      (dolist (tag (@tag-list p))
        (push p (gethash tag by-tag))))
    (maphash (lambda (k v) (puthash k (nreverse v) by-tag)) by-tag)
    by-tag))

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(defun blog--publishable-p (file)
  "Return non-nil if FILE is an article that should be published.
A file is publishable when it has any of:
  - #+modified:                 ---standalone post
  - #+article_style: multiple   ---container of subtree articles
  - #+site_nav:                 ---top-level nav page (e.g. about.org)

Fragments that are #+include'd into articles but never published on
their own (e.g. MathJaxPreamble.org) must opt out explicitly with

  #+exclude_from_publish: t

Any other .org file missing /both/ a publish marker and the opt-out
is stamped with #+modified: <today>, below its leading #-lines, and
published ---a stray scratch file becomes a fresh article, not a
failed build."
  (with-temp-buffer
    (insert-file-contents file)
    (cl-flet ((kw (k) (blog--file-keyword nil k)))
      (cond
       ((kw "exclude_from_publish") nil)
       ((kw "modified") t)
       ((equal (kw "article_style") "multiple") t)
       ((kw "site_nav") t)
       (t (let ((today (format-time-string "%Y-%m-%d")))
            ;; Below the keyword block, so a leading “-*- … -*-” line stays first.
            (goto-char (point-min))
            (while (looking-at "#") (forward-line))
            (insert (format "#+modified: %s\n" today))
            (write-region nil nil file)
            (when-let ((buf (get-file-buffer file)))
              (with-current-buffer buf
                (unless (buffer-modified-p) (revert-buffer t t t))))
            (message "%s had no #+modified:; stamped it %s" (f-filename file) today)
            t))))))

(defun blog-publish-all ()
  "Batch-publish every article and regenerate the index.  The sole CI entry point.

Exports all posts → public/, rebuilds index + tag pages + RSS, copies static
assets.  public/ is then deployed by CI to gh-pages so URLs stay flat:
alhassy.com/foo not alhassy.com/public/foo.

Dispatches on #+article_style: per file:
  standalone (default) → one .org → one HTML file,
  multiple            → one subtree → one HTML file (via `blog--publish-multiple-articles')."
  ;; CI passes relative directories, e.g., "./public/"; pin them now, else a nested article's
  ;; buffer would resolve them against its own subdirectory.
  (let ((blog-posts-directory   (expand-file-name blog-posts-directory))
        (blog-publish-directory (file-name-as-directory (expand-file-name blog-publish-directory))))
    (make-directory blog-publish-directory t)
    ;; Vendor every :REDIRECT: first, so the posts registry reads each source's metadata ---say, its image--- fresh.
    (dolist (f (blog--org-files))
      (when (equal (blog--file-keyword f "article_style") "multiple")
        (dolist (info (blog--info-multiple f))
          (when-let ((redirect (map-elt info "redirect")))
            (blog--vendor-one-redirect (or (map-elt info "redirect-slug") (map-elt info "slug")) redirect)))))
    (blog--refresh-posts)
    (blog--validate-slugs)
    (dolist (f (seq-filter #'blog--publishable-p (blog--org-files)))
      (with-current-buffer (find-file-noselect f)
        ;; badge:/doc:/tweet: links in articles are registered as Org link types
        ;; by org-special-block-extras-mode.  It's a buffer-local minor mode, so
        ;; turn it on per buffer ---without it those links leak verbatim to HTML.
        (org-special-block-extras-mode 1)
        (if (blog--multiple-style-p)
            (progn
              (message "=> Exporting all articles from %s..." (f-base f))
              (blog--publish-multiple-articles f))
          (let ((target (expand-file-name (concat (f-base f) ".html") blog-publish-directory)))
            ;; Export directly to public/<base>.html so that #+export_file_name:
            ;; entries pointing at ~/blog/ (valid locally, absent on CI) never
            ;; become the output destination.
            (org-export-to-file 'blog target)))))
    (blog-make-index-page)
    (blog--make-rss-feed)
    (blog--sync-assets)))

*** How the export pipeline connects the two styles
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-the-export-pipeline-connects-the-two-styles
:END:

The =blog= backend reads an article's metadata from file-level =#+keyword:= lines, via
=blog--info=; a container heading keeps its metadata in a =:PROPERTIES:= drawer instead.
So =blog--publish-single-subtree= turns the heading into a /synthetic temp file/:

1. =blog--info-subtree= reads the heading's metadata.
2. The temp file gets the matching file-level keywords ---=#+title:=, =#+filetags:=,
   =#+fileimage:=, =#+description:=--- plus =#+history_url:= and =#+htmlized_source_url:=,
   pointing the badges at the container's git log and the per-article colourised source.
3. The subtree body is pasted in and promoted a level; e.g., =** Abstract :ignore:= ↦
   =* Abstract :ignore:=, just where =blog--style-setup= looks for it.
4. We export with the =blog= backend, as for any standalone post.
5. A narrowed copy of the subtree is htmlized into =<slug>.org.html=.

Both styles thus share one export pipeline; they differ only in how they reach it.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(defun blog--htmlize-subtree (heading-point slug)
  "Htmlize the subtree at HEADING-POINT in the current buffer to ~/blog/SLUG.org.html.

This produces a per-article colourised source view for container sub-articles.
We copy the subtree content to a temp buffer, narrow to the pasted content,
htmlize, and write the result."
  (save-excursion
    (goto-char heading-point)
    (org-copy-subtree))
  (with-temp-buffer
    (org-mode)
    (org-paste-subtree 1)
    (let ((buffer-file-name (concat slug ".org")))
      (blog--htmlize-to slug))))


(defun blog--publish-single-subtree (heading-point container-file info)
  "Export the subtree at HEADING-POINT in the current buffer to `blog-publish-directory'/SLUG.html.

INFO is the pre-resolved metadata alist for this heading (a single entry
from `blog--info-multiple').  The slug is read from INFO.

The subtree is copied into a temp .org file populated with synthetic
file-level keywords so that the `blog' backend exports it as a standalone article."
  (let* ((slug    (@slug info))
         (tmp-org (make-temp-file "albasmala-" nil ".org"))
         (tmp-buf (find-file-noselect tmp-org)))
    (unwind-protect
        (progn
          ;; 1. Populate temp file with synthetic file-level keywords.
          ;;    blog--info reads these via regex when the `blog' backend calls
          ;;    (blog--info buffer-file-name) during export.
          (with-current-buffer tmp-buf
            (erase-buffer)
            (insert
             "#+title: "                (cdr (assoc "title"       info)) "\n"
             "#+modified: "            (cdr (assoc "date"         info)) "\n"
             "#+fileimage: "           (cdr (assoc "image"        info)) "\n"
             "#+filetags: "            (cdr (assoc "tags"         info)) "\n"
             "#+description: "        (cdr (assoc "description"  info)) "\n"
             (if (equal "t" (cdr (assoc "draft" info))) "#+draft: t\n" "")
             ;; Synthetic overrides ---blog--info prefers these over auto-derived values.
             "#+history_url: "         (cdr (assoc "history"      info)) "\n"
             "#+htmlized_source_url: " (blog--url slug ".org.html") "\n"
             "\n")
            (save-buffer))

          ;; 2. Populate the temp file body.
          ;;
          ;;    When the heading carries a :REDIRECT: property we simply emit a
          ;;    #+include: directive pointing at the external file ---Org's own
          ;;    include machinery handles the rest during export.
          ;;
          ;;    Otherwise we copy the subtree, paste it, axe the redundant
          ;;    top-level heading line, and promote all children one level so
          ;;    blog--style-setup's "^* Abstract" search finds them correctly:
          ;;
          ;;      Before promotion   →   After
          ;;      * Article Title        (deleted)
          ;;      ** Abstract :ignore:   * Abstract :ignore:
          ;;      ** Introduction ...    * Introduction ...
          (let ((redirect      (cdr (assoc "redirect" info)))
                (redirect-slug (or (cdr (assoc "redirect-slug" info)) slug)))
            (if redirect
                (with-current-buffer tmp-buf
                  (goto-char (point-max))
                  ;; Vendor afresh, so the #+include: resolves on this machine and on
                  ;; CI, and tracks the source's tip; the banner sits atop the article.
                  ;; A package's write-up uses the package's own link types; load it first,
                  ;; unless it's already loaded ---say, our working copy, mid-hack.
                  (when-let ((lib (cdr (assoc "load" info))))
                    (unless (featurep (intern (file-name-base lib)))
                      (load (blog--redirect-load-file redirect-slug redirect lib) nil t)))
                  ;; An article of a redirected container is its heading's contents, read with the
                  ;; container's file-level keywords ---say, its #+macro:s and header-args.
                  (let ((vendored (expand-file-name (blog--vendor-one-redirect redirect-slug redirect) blog-posts-directory)))
                    (insert (blog--redirect-banner redirect)
                            (if (assoc "redirect-slug" info)
                                (format "#+setupfile: %s\n#+include: \"%s::#%s\" :only-contents t :minlevel 1\n"
                                        vendored vendored slug)
                              (format "#+include: \"%s\"\n" vendored))))
                  (save-buffer))
              (save-excursion
                (goto-char heading-point)
                (org-copy-subtree))
              (with-current-buffer tmp-buf
                (goto-char (point-max))
                (org-paste-subtree 1)
                (goto-char (point-min))
                (when (re-search-forward "^\\* " nil t)
                  (delete-region (line-beginning-position) (line-beginning-position 2)))
                (org-map-entries #'org-promote t)
                (save-buffer))))

          ;; 3. Export with the `blog' backend.
          ;;    Write straight to public/<slug>.html, mirroring the standalone path in
          ;;    `blog-publish-all'; nothing else copies it there, so CI would never deploy it.
          (let* ((html-out (expand-file-name (concat slug ".html") blog-publish-directory)))
            (with-current-buffer tmp-buf
              (let ((blog--link-base-directory (file-name-directory (expand-file-name container-file))))
                (org-export-to-file 'blog html-out))))

          ;; 4. Per-article colourised source: the vendored file for a redirect, else the subtree.
          (if (cdr (assoc "redirect" info))
              (blog--htmlize-file (expand-file-name (concat blog-redirects-subdir (or (cdr (assoc "redirect-slug" info)) slug) ".org")
                                                    blog-posts-directory))
            (blog--htmlize-subtree heading-point slug)))

      ;; Cleanup temp files regardless of errors.
      ;; Mark the buffer unmodified before killing ---this is the only reliable way
      ;; to prevent "Buffer modified, kill anyway?" prompts regardless of what
      ;; kill-buffer-query-functions contains.
      (when (buffer-live-p tmp-buf)
        (with-current-buffer tmp-buf (set-buffer-modified-p nil))
        (kill-buffer tmp-buf))
      (when (file-exists-p tmp-org) (delete-file tmp-org)))))
#+end_src

(defun blog--publish-multiple-articles (container-file)
  "Publish each top-level heading of CONTAINER-FILE as a separate HTML article.

Writes the derived slug back to the heading as a :CUSTOM_ID: property so future
publishes are stable even if the heading title changes.

Returns the list of slugs that were published."
  (let* ((all-infos (blog--info-multiple container-file))
         ;; A heading's infos are usually just its own; a :REDIRECT: to a container yields its articles.
         (by-slug   (let ((h (make-hash-table :test #'equal)))
                      (dolist (i (reverse all-infos)) (push i (gethash (or (map-elt i "redirect-slug") (@slug i)) h)))
                      h))
         (results   '()))
    (with-current-buffer (find-file-noselect container-file)
      (save-excursion
        (goto-char (point-min))
        (while (re-search-forward "^\\* " nil t)
          (beginning-of-line)
          (let* ((tags  (mapcar #'downcase (org-get-tags)))
                 (title (org-get-heading t t t t))
                 (slug  (org-entry-get (point) "CUSTOM_ID")))
            (unless (or (member "noexport" tags)
                        (org-element-property :commentedp (org-element-at-point)))
              (unless slug
                (user-error "Container heading %S in %s has no :CUSTOM_ID: property ---run C-x C-s on the source first"
                            title (f-filename container-file)))
              (dolist (info (gethash slug by-slug))
                (message "=> Publishing subtree: %s (%s)..." (@title info) (@slug info))
                (blog--publish-single-subtree (point) container-file info)
                (push (@slug info) results))))
          (org-end-of-subtree t t))))
    (nreverse results)))

** Deployment via CI
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: deployment-via-ci
:END:

We keep two branches: =master= holds the sources, and CI owns all the HTML.

#+begin_example
  master branch                      gh-pages branch
  ──────────────────────────────     ─────────────────────────────────
  foo.org         (source)      ──►  foo.html     (alhassy.com/foo)
  AlBasmala.org   (source)   CI      index.html
  resources/                    ──►  tag-emacs.html
  AlBasmala.el                       tag-programming-language.html
  (NO .html files here)              rss.xml
                                     resources/  ...
#+end_example

- =master= holds only sources: =.org= files, =resources/=, and config.
- Every push makes CI export every post, =index.html=, the =tag-*.html= pages,
  and =rss.xml= into =public/=, alongside a copy of =resources/=.
- =peaceiris/actions-gh-pages= force-pushes =public/= to =gh-pages=, which
  GitHub Pages serves at the root; so URLs are flat: =alhassy.com/foo=.

*CI rebuilds everything, every run.* A fresh checkout has no =public/=, so
=blog-publish-all= exports every publishable =.org= ---no mtimes, no diffing---
and the deploy overwrites =gh-pages= wholesale.

Hence a check either runs before any HTML is written, or not at all.
=blog--validate-slugs= runs on =C-x C-s=, where slug clashes are cheapest to
fix, and first thing in =blog-publish-all=; so a pushed-but-never-previewed
edit fails the build rather than silently overwriting one page with another.

- *What is deployed?* Browse the =gh-pages= branch; it is exactly what
  =alhassy.com= serves.
- *One-time setup:* in the repo's Settings → Pages, set /Source/ to "Deploy
  from a branch", with branch =gh-pages= and folder =/=. Otherwise GitHub
  Pages serves =master=, where no HTML lives.
- *Timing:* a run takes ~2 minutes, of which exporting is ~30 s; the rest is
  provisioning a runner and installing Emacs. Public repos get unlimited
  Actions minutes, so this costs nothing.

(defun blog--sync-assets ()
  "Copy static assets into blog-publish-directory so relative HTML paths resolve.

Called at the end of `blog-publish-all' so that public/ is self-contained and

  (let ((dist (file-name-as-directory (expand-file-name blog-publish-directory))))

    (dolist (asset '("readremaining.js-readremainingjs" "floating-toc.css"))

        (when (file-exists-p src)
          (if (file-directory-p src)
              (copy-directory src (expand-file-name asset dist) t t t)
            (copy-file src (expand-file-name asset dist) t)))))))

#+begin_details
1. Go to your repo: https://github.com/alhassy/alhassy.github.io/settings/pages
2. Add a =Custom Domain= such as an "apex domain" like =alhassy.com= (or a www domain like =www.alhassy.com=)
   - Apex domains require =A= records to be setup on your DNS provider.
   - www domains require =CNAME= records.
   - On my DNS provider, I setup both: That way, with or without =www.=, people will arrive at my blog.
3. Go to your DNS provider, and add two records
   | Type  | Name | Priority | Content           |   TTL |
   |-------+------+----------+-------------------+-------|
   | CNAME | www  |        0 | alhassy.github.io | 14400 |
   | CNAME | @    |        0 | alhassy.github.io | 14400 |
4. In a terminal, run: =dig www.alhassy.com +nostats +nocomments +nocmd=
   - The =www.= is intentional.

5. You should see something like:
    #+begin_example bash
    www.alhassy.com.    14400   IN      CNAME   alhassy.github.io.
    alhassy.github.io.  3600    IN      A       185.199.111.153
    alhassy.github.io.  3600    IN      A       185.199.108.153
    alhassy.github.io.  3600    IN      A       185.199.109.153
    alhassy.github.io.  3600    IN      A       185.199.110.153
    #+end_example

    This says that it may take 3600 seconds, or 1hour, for the redirect of ~alhassy.com~ to
    ~alhassy.github.io~ to be completed. It may take longer; keep reading.

6. In your DNS provider, add 4 records, one for each IP Address you got from the =dig= command.
   (These addresses are also on the official Github docs).

   | Type | Name | Priority |         Content |   TTL |
   |------+------+----------+-----------------+-------|
   | A    | @    |        0 | 185.199.108.153 | 14400 |
   | A    | @    |        0 | 185.199.109.153 | 14400 |
   | A    | @    |        0 | 185.199.110.153 | 14400 |
   | A    | @    |        0 | 185.199.111.153 | 14400 |

7. Run =dig alhassy.com +noall +answer -t A= and ensure it points to these IP addresses.
   - Notice the lack of a =www.=

8. Open an incognito browser, private browsing session, and navigate to =alhassy.com=.
   a. If this redirects to your =alhassy.github.io= blog, then joy!
      - If the redirect does not happen in your non-incognito browser, just clear your browsing history and try again.
   b. Otherwise, it may take some time (something like 1/2hour to 3 days) for the DNS propagation
      to be completed.
      - You can check the progress by using a service like this.
      - It took me about 1/2hour for the URL to redirect to my github.io blog.

(defun get-raw-and-commits (url)
  "
  Given a github 'url', return the associated commits history and raw textual urls,
  as a dotted pair.

  For example,
  url     → https://github.com/⟪user⟫/⟪project⟫/blob/master/content/⟪filepath⟫
  raw     → https://raw.githubusercontent.com/⟪user⟫/⟪project⟫/master/content/⟪filepath⟫
  commits → https://github.com/⟪user⟫/⟪project⟫/commits/master/content/⟪filepath⟫
  "

  (let* ((github  "https://github.com/")
     (comm  (s-split "/" (s-chop-prefix github url)))
       )

      (setf (nth 2 comm) "commits")

      ;; raw, then commits
      `(
       , (s-prepend "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/"
         (s-replace "/blob/" "/" (s-chop-prefix github url)))
       .
       ,(s-prepend github (s-join "/" comm))
       )
  )
)

(defun make-top-matter ()
  "This is the top-most text that appears right after the article's
   title. It includes viewing the source, a PDF rendition, and
   the most recent date of modification --unless the variables are nil.
  "
  (let* ((date (format-time-string "%Y-%m-%d"))
     (content "")
     (rawsrc  (car (get-raw-and-commits org.sourcefile)))
     (commits (cdr (get-raw-and-commits org.sourcefile)))
    )

     ;; Perform the loop over tuples (constraint url description).
     (dolist (var `( (,org.nopdf ,blogrepo-file.pdf  "Read as PDF"   )
             (,org.nopdf nil                 " or "          )
             (nil ,rawsrc                    "See the source")
             (,org.nomodificationdate nil
                ,(concat " ; " (unless org.nopdf "<br>")))
             (,org.nomodificationdate ,commits "Last modified")
             (,org.nomodificationdate nil ,(concat " on " date))
           )
          content)
      ;; Unless there are constraints, concatenate the resulting html.
      (unless (car var) (setq content (concat content (make-html-link (cadr var) (caddr var)))))
     )

     ;; for debugging / drafting,
     (concat
       (when org.draft (format "<center> Draft: %s </center>" (gensym)))
       "<small> <center> ⟨ " content " ⟩ </center> </small>")
  )

***** ~preview-article~ -- the heart of ~AlBasmala.el~
   :PROPERTIES:
   :CUSTOM_ID: preview-article-the-heart-of-albasmala-el
   :END:

 We make the article in stages:
  0. Go to the Org source and use the native Org utitlies to produce a coloured html file.
  1. Insert the article image into that html file.
     - We do so *before* producing the Jekyll markdown variant so that we can preview it correctly.
  2. Remove some clutter from the html, yielding a markdown file.
  3. Prepend the Jekyll header created using the keywords.
  4. Move the markdown file to the ~_posts~ directory and show the html file in a browser.

 :Nope:
 We use ~toggle~, a personal function from my ~init~,
 that toggles a variables value till the end of its form.
 We use it below to disable all Emacs buffer local variables, do some work,
 then re-enable them afterwards. Such variables generally require a query
 since they could be dangerous, like erasing the disk, so we disable them temporarily.
 :End:

 #+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp :tangle AlBasmala.el
(local-set-key (kbd "<f7>") 'preview-article)

  (cl-defun preview-article (&key (browser nil) (draft nil))
    "Create and preview a the html form of the content.

    A non-nil value for "org.nopdf" short-circuits the generation of a PDF,
    thereby yielding a possibly faster execution.

    A non-nil value for ":browser" opens the article using the default browser.
    This may be undesirable, since it may open many tabs in your brower.

    The 'draft' keyword option is here in case we want to override
    whatever the local '#+DRAFT' value may be.
    "

    (interactive)
    (save-buffer)

    (ensure-blog-is-serving)

    ;; Remove any existing html, in case we fail to generate it
    ;; we do not want to render an out of date version.
    (shell-command (concat "rm ~/alhassy.github.io/_posts/" jekyll.name.md))

    (setq enable-local-variables nil)
    (setq enable-local-eval nil)

    ;; compile coloured html
    (find-file file.org)
    (GetOrgKeyWords)
    (when draft (setq org.draft draft))
    (org-html-export-to-html)

    ;; Insert image, duh.
    (insert-image-and-other-formats)

    ;; Discard first 3 lines, (note the 1-indexing), since they don't look very nice
    ;; in the resulting markdown file when rendered on the Jekyll site.
    (shell-command (concat "tail -n +4 <" file.html " >" jekyll.name.md))

    ;; Preprend file with a header.
    (find-file jekyll.name.md)
    (beginning-of-buffer)
    (MakeHeader)
    (insert HEADER)
    (save-buffer)
    (kill-buffer jekyll.name.md)

    ;; Move it to posts directory.
    (shell-command (concat "mv " jekyll.name.md " " blogrepo-posts))

    ;; ;; Uncomment for debugging.
    ;; ;; (find-file (concat "~/alhassy.github.io/_posts/" jekyll.name.md))

    ;; no pdf generation in draft mode
    (unless (or org.draft org.nopdf) (my-org-latex-export-to-pdf))

    ;; Preview locally in browser.
    (when browser
      (let* ((buf (concat "*AlBasmala*" NAME "*")))

        (toggle kill-buffer-query-functions nil (ignore-errors (kill-buffer buf)))
        (async-shell-command (concat "open http://localhost:4000/" NAME "/") buf)
      )
    )

    (message "Article has been opened in your browser.")

    (setq enable-local-variables t)
    (setq enable-local-eval t)
  )
