1. Create a Routine and Stick to It

Imagine your daily routine as the framework of a building—the stronger and more consistent it is, the higher and more confidently you can build. It's the sum of your habits that gives your day its shape, its predictability, and its rhythm.

Think of it this way: a solid daily routine is your secret weapon for success. Every tweak and improvement, even as small as shaving off 10 minutes from a task, adds up big over time.

Save those 10 minutes a day, and suddenly you've got an extra 60 hours a year. That's like a little time treasure you just found under the couch cushions.

But how does a routine actually make you work smarter? It's simple. A routine takes the guesswork out of your day.

Instead of starting each morning wondering what you'll tackle first, you have a plan. That saves mental energy right off the bat.

Then there's the time saved from not having to make a bunch of decisions—because let's be honest, decision-making is exhausting.

A well-crafted routine aligns your daily actions with your energy levels. Slot in high-concentration tasks when your mind is fresh, and save the autopilot stuff for when your brain is ready for a break. That way, you're always playing to your strengths.

And routines aren't just about work. They encompass everything from your morning jog to the wind-down ritual before bed. It's all about creating patterns that maximize your time and energy.

3. Daily Morning Ritual End Prompt & Weekly habit check: Are ya happy?

Ask “If today were the last day of my life, would I do what I'm about to do today?”

Whenever the answer has been no for too many days in a row, it's time to change something.

Add a "PositivityScoreXXX" tag to each day to quickly gauge how positive a day was. Score is calculated as a sum:

Did I do my routines? 1 point for each routine.

Did I get my scheduled goals done? 2 points for each task.

How is my mood: depressed -3; down -2; okay -1; good +1; great +2; fantastic +3. In the morning use this scale but -1 from absolute value; in the afternoon use the scale as is.

Anxious -1; not anxious +1

Smiled all day or tried to do so +1.

This is a combination of my :doableDay: tag and my :moodXXX: tag.

The idea is that you can assign a positive or negative score to everything that happened during the day and if the score at the end of the day is above 1, it counts as a good day. You can also track your streaks/habits, i.e routines followed, another day sober etc. and set whether you want the score of these habits to slowly increase, stay the same or decrease.

By default, the score would start off on 1.0. But if you're a generally happy/unhappy person, and you can equate a score to your mood, you can set that score as your starting point for the day as something to work towards.
